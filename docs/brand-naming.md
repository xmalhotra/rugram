# Brand naming: core, rules and shortlist

Status: working document, 25 October 2026. Conflicts below come from web searches only. Nothing here is a trademark clearance; the chosen name still needs a professional Indian trademark search (classes 9, 35, 41 and 42) and checks of the App Store, Play Store, domains and social handles.

**Chosen name: Qram.** The icon is **क्र.** The rebrand steps are in `docs/qram-rebrand.md`. Bahaav and Kunji remain the earlier recommendations.

## 1. Brand core

The app is **the friend who's figured it out**.

For urban Indians aged roughly 20 to 35, it shows how to do everyday things properly (Flows, the hero feature), what is actually worth buying (Picks) and where to go (Events). After a week, people should feel **smart and in the know**: making better choices without being lectured.

This came from four answers:

- Personality: a knowledgeable friend or older sibling, not a coach, guru or doctor.
- Feeling: smart and in the know.
- Known for, three years out: Flows (visual routines and guides).
- First audience: urban Indians, 20 to 35.

## 2. Naming rules

A name passes only if it:

1. **Sounds like a friend talking.** Casual and Hinglish-friendly. Formal or spiritual words (Shrey, Sopan, Kramsh) fail this, because they sound like a teacher.
2. **Hints at know-how**: knowing how, having the key, having it figured out.
3. **Is short and soft.** Ideally two syllables, no harsh consonant clusters, easy to say and to spell after hearing it once.
4. **Is ownable.** Not already used by an app or brand in a nearby space.
5. **Leaves room to grow.** Routines, supplements, salts, gums, cards, events, new cities. Nothing medical, nothing tied to "habits".
6. **Avoids bad readings.** No preachy, mocking, religious, medical or adult associations in Hindi or English.

## 3. What the checks showed

About 60 names were built and tested (full list in the appendix). Two patterns stood out:

- **The words that fit best are already taken, mostly by nearby apps.** Funda (a Hindi daily-life tips app from Testbook), Psst (two lifestyle apps covering habits, picks, deals and events), Sortd (a shopping-wishlist app with about 700,000 users), HoshiYaar (a kids' learning app), Ai se (an Indian AI life-coach app). The idea is right; others got to the obvious words first.
- **Short invented words are crowded too.** Fynda, Tryka, Sleeka, Kiyo and Tryday are all in use. Tryka is a Hyrox-style fitness race brand, a direct clash with the Flows content.

So the realistic choice is between a less obvious real word with a strong story, and a built word with some remaining risk. The three below are the best fit to the brand core with the fewest conflicts found.

## 4. Shortlist

### Recommended: Kunji (कुंजी)

- **Say it:** KOON-jee.
- **Meaning:** "key" in Hindi. It is also what Indian students called the guide book with all the answers in it. The friend who's figured it out is the one who has the kunji.
- **Why it fits:**
  - **Friend, not teacher.** A kunji is what a friend slips you; teachers disapproved. That cheekiness is the brand's voice.
  - **Smart and in the know.** It literally means having the key, or the answers.
  - **Flows.** A kunji is a step-by-step guide, which is exactly what a Flow is.
  - **Scales.** "The kunji to credit cards", "the kunji to electrolytes", "the kunji to Saturday in Hyderabad" all work.
  - **Sound.** Two soft syllables, open ending, easy anywhere.
  - **Nostalgia.** Most of the 20 to 35 audience grew up with kunjis.
- **Branding starter:**
  - Wordmark: **kunji** in lowercase, with the dot of the "j" shaped as a keyhole.
  - Icon: a key whose teeth form a small staircase, a key and a next step in one shape.
  - Tagline: "Kunji. Figured out, for you." Avoid "key to everything", which another Kunji app already uses.
  - Voice: a friend's tip, often opening with "Ek kaam kar…" (here's what you do).
- **Risks found:**
  - KUNJI, a Pakistan and UK community-management app (visitors, deliveries, complaints) on both stores, listed under Lifestyle on the App Store. Different service, same name; the App Store needs a unique listing name, so plan on something like "Kunji: Flows, Picks, Events".
  - A Mumbai fintech called Kunji appears in a 2022 startup directory; its current status is unknown.
  - Some people may associate kunjis with cramming. Most will read it as a clever shortcut.
- **Risk level:** medium. The strongest fit, so it is worth paying for a trademark search on it first.

### Second: Sleeqa

- **Say it:** SLEE-ka.
- **Meaning:** a respelling of *saleeqa* (सलीक़ा), Hindi-Urdu for taste, finesse, the knack of doing things properly. English readers see "sleek" in it.
- **Why it fits:** the friend with good taste and the right way of doing things. Strongest on Picks, good on Flows (proper form) and Events (good taste in plans). Works internationally.
- **Branding starter:** lowercase **sleeqa** with the tail of the "q" drawn as a smooth swoosh. Tagline: "Sleeqa. The good way to do it."
- **Risks found:** a small online store called Sleeqa (scissors) and a London streetwear brand called Saleeqa. The bigger problem is spelling: people who hear it will type "Sleeka", which is a postpartum shapewear brand.
- **Risk level:** low to medium. Less friend-like than Kunji; more polished.

### Third: KnowBetter

- **Say it:** know-BET-er.
- **Meaning:** from "When you know better, you do better." It keeps "Better" from BetterHabits, so existing users get continuity.
- **Why it fits:** smart and in the know, clear to everyone, works globally from day one.
- **Branding starter:** **KnowBetter** with the two words in different weights. Tagline: "Know better. Do better."
- **Risks found:** a US web studio called KnowBetter Creative (since 1999) and a security-training product called KnowBetter. As a common phrase it is harder to register, and it can sound like a telling-off ("you should know better").
- **Risk level:** medium. The safest to understand, the weakest to own.

## 5. How to choose

1. **Five-friend test.** Say each name once, out loud, to five people in the target audience, without showing it written down. Check three things: can they say it back, can they spell it, and do they remember it the next day? Then tell them what the app does and ask which name fits.
2. **Lock the basics for the winner** the same day: domains (for example `.app`, `.in`, `get…com`), Instagram and X handles, and a search of both app stores.
3. **Professional trademark search** in India for classes 9, 35, 41 and 42 before any public use.
4. **Then rename the codebase**: display name, slug, deep-link scheme, iOS and Android identifiers (cheap to change before the first store release), and the environment-variable aliases already in place for the BetterHabits rename.

## 6. Flow-led round

Flows are the feature the app should be known for, so this round looked for "flow" itself: Hindi, Urdu, Arabic, Sanskrit, Japanese, Spanish and English. The plain English word is not ownable. Flo is a period-tracking app with hundreds of millions of users, and "Flow" is already a generic app category.

### Recommended from this round: Bahaav (बहाव)

- **Say it:** ba-HAAV.
- **Meaning:** a current, the way water moves. In everyday Hindi it is also how things feel when they are going smoothly.
- **Why it fits the product:** a Flow is one movement leading into the next, which is what बहाव means. Picks and Events are what you let into the flow of the day, so the name still covers them. It is not medical and not limited to habits.
- **Branding starter:**
  - Wordmark: lowercase **bahaav**. The two a's can be drawn as one continuous loop, so the spelling itself looks like a flow.
  - Icon: a single smooth wave that also reads as a short path.
  - Tagline: "Bahaav. Your day, in flow." In Hindi: "बहाव में आ जाओ."
  - In the product, one routine can be called a bahaav, so the feature name and the brand are the same word.
- **Risks found:**
  - No consumer app under this spelling. What exists is a small Delhi fashion partnership registered in 2026, a Nepali film, and a Gurgaon wedding band.
  - Spelling is the real risk. Drop one "a" and it becomes **Bhaav** (भाव), a different word meaning price or feeling. That spelling is used by a mood journal, a bullion-rates app and a social app. People must be taught the double "a", and the close spellings should be registered too.
- **Risk level:** low to medium. Best flow word found that is not already an app.

### Spellings asked about after Bahaav

- **Vhaav.** Drops the ब from बहाव, so it is no longer the word. The "vh" start is hard to say, and it sits next to Vaave, Vahak and Vahan.
- **Krhm.** Meant as करम with the vowels taken out, the way सक्षम became Kshm. करम is क-र-म, so the consonants are krm. The ह in krhm is not in the word. Krm is the name already checked as Kram. The full word Karam is also taken: KARAM Safety, a large Indian PPE company, has its own app on both stores. The same spelling is read as कर्म, which sounds like a lesson.

### Suggestion on the kram root

The word worth claiming is **क्रम** (sequence, one thing after another), which is the flow. The brand spelling is **Kram**. Say it the way Hindi says क्रम से, one beat. Put **क्रम** on the icon so the word is not read as कर्म or करम.

Tagline: "क्रम से." A routine inside the app is one kram.

Longer forms were checked and are already companies: Krama (Krama AI, a workflow product that turns work into habits), Karam (KARAM Safety), Kramse (the KarmSe task app in Pune), Anukrama (a Bangalore IT firm, anukrama.com), Upakram (a UK software consultancy, upakram.com). Kram itself still matches a US study app and a Jaipur agency, so it needs a trademark search before it replaces Bahaav.

- **Qram.** A Q in place of the क. क is a K, so the Hindi word disappears, and English reads Q as "kw". Checked again on 25 October 2026: qram.in is free to register. The old Jaipur QR attendance app that used this name is gone from the Play Store, and the directory page that still cites qram.in is stale. A store search on 25 October 2026 found no live app titled Qram on the Play Store or the App Store. Nearby names are QGram, QRGram and QRamen. Neither store locks a name in advance, so a later trademark complaint can still remove it. The acronym QRAM is still used by an Australian asset-tracking system and by Queensland Remote Aboriginal Media, so the four letters keep reading as QR. The icon is **क्र.** — the conjunct plus a full stop, drawn as one custom mark. The dot is part of the logo and stands in for the remaining म in क्रम. The Latin word sits outside the icon.
